On-page SEO Checklist

On-page SEO is everything you control inside a page. Getting it right tells Google and visitors exactly what the page is about.

🏷️

Title tag

Put the main keyword near the start, keep it under about 60 characters and make it unique on every page. It is the biggest on-page signal and the headline in search results.

Quick check: search the page title in Google and read how it looks.
📄

Meta description

Write a clear summary under about 155 characters. It does not rank pages directly, but a good one raises clicks.

Quick check: every page has its own description.
🧱

Headings

Use one H1, then H2 and H3 in a logical order. Headings help readers scan and help search engines understand structure.

Quick check: open the page outline and check there is only one H1.
🎯

Search intent

Match what the searcher wants: an answer, a comparison or a product. A page that fits intent keeps visitors and ranks better.

Quick check: read the top 5 results for your keyword first.
🖼️

Images

Use descriptive file names, alt text and compressed sizes. This helps image search and page speed.

Quick check: no image is missing alt text.
🔗

Internal links

Link related pages with descriptive anchor text. This spreads authority and helps people reach more pages.

Quick check: every page links to at least 2 related pages.
🧾

URL structure

Keep URLs short, readable and built around the primary keyword, with hyphens between words. Avoid dates, IDs and long parameters, and never change a live URL without a 301 redirect.

Quick check: each URL is short and describes the page.
🏆

Content quality and E-E-A-T

Show real experience, expertise and trust: a named author, accurate facts, original examples and sources. Content that fully answers the query is the strongest on-page signal.

Quick check: a real person could vouch for every claim on the page.

Keyword strategy: research, clusters, mapping and cannibalization

Good rankings start with choosing the right keywords and giving each one a single home page.

How to do keyword research

Keyword research finds the words and questions your customers type into Google, so you can build pages around them.

  • Always judge a keyword by intent first, then by volume.
  • Prefer realistic keywords over the biggest ones, especially for a newer site.
  1. Seed topics

    List your products, services and the questions customers ask.

  2. Expand

    Use the Semrush Keyword Magic Tool, Google autocomplete, People Also Ask and Search Console queries.

  3. Check the numbers

    Look at search volume, keyword difficulty, CPC and the trend over time.

  4. Read the intent

    Search the keyword and read the top 5 results: guides, comparisons, products or brand pages?

  5. Choose targets

    Pick keywords that match the page's purpose and are realistic for your site's authority.

  6. Record

    Save them in a sheet, then cluster and map them using the next sections.

What is keyword clustering?

Keyword clustering means grouping related keywords that share the same search intent, so one page can target the whole group instead of many thin pages.

  • Group by intent first, then by topic.
  • SERP overlap test: if the same 3 or more URLs rank for two keywords, they belong on one page.
  • Choose one primary keyword and list the rest as secondary.
  • Tools such as Semrush can cluster a list automatically, but always review the result by hand.

What is keyword mapping?

Keyword mapping assigns each keyword cluster to one specific URL. It becomes your plan for what every page should rank for.

  • One primary keyword per page.
  • Two pages never share the same primary keyword.
  • Clusters with no page are gaps, so they become new pages.
  • Clusters with two pages are overlaps, so fix cannibalization.

What is keyword cannibalization?

Keyword cannibalization happens when two or more pages on your site target the same keyword or intent, so they compete with each other instead of with your rivals.

  • Signs: rankings jump between two URLs, the wrong page ranks, or a page stays stuck.
  • Find it: in Search Console open Performance, click a query, then the Pages tab. The Semrush Position Tracking cannibalization report also shows it.
  • Fix 1: merge the pages and 301 redirect the weaker one.
  • Fix 2: give one page a different intent and update its title and headings.
  • Fix 3: link internally to the page you want to rank, using the keyword as anchor text.
  • For duplicate URLs of one page, use canonical tags. See also the Easyquickgrade case study.

AEO and GEO: SEO for the AI era

More people now get answers straight from Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini and voice assistants. AEO and GEO help your content get picked as that answer.

AEO: Answer Engine Optimization

AEO means shaping your content so answer engines can lift a short, direct answer from it. These include Google featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes, AI Overviews and voice assistants.

  • Use a question as a heading, such as "What is a sitemap?".
  • Answer it directly in the first 40 to 60 words, then explain.
  • Use short lists, steps and tables, which are easy to quote.
  • Add an FAQ section for real questions people ask.
  • Add FAQPage or HowTo schema so machines can read the structure (see schema markup).
  • Keep one clear idea per section.
Example: Heading "What is robots.txt?" followed by one clear sentence: "robots.txt is a text file that tells search engine crawlers which pages of a site they may visit."

GEO: Generative Engine Optimization

GEO means making your brand and pages likely to be cited or mentioned inside answers written by AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. These tools pick sources they can trust and understand.

  • Show expertise: real author, real experience, clear about page (E-E-A-T).
  • Add original data, statistics and credible sources. Early research suggests this raises visibility in AI answers.
  • Write clearly structured, up-to-date pages that cover a topic in depth.
  • Keep your name, brand and details identical across your site, LinkedIn and listings.
  • Earn mentions on trusted, relevant sites, not only links.
  • Let AI crawlers in and add llms.txt.
  • Track it: Semrush shows AI visibility, mentions and cited pages.

Common mistakes

  • Using the same title on many pages.
  • Stuffing keywords instead of writing for people.
  • Leaving important pages with no internal links.
  • Ignoring search intent.
  • Writing for search engines instead of people.

Frequently asked questions

What is on-page SEO?

On-page SEO is optimising the content and HTML of a page, such as titles, headings, copy, images and internal links, so search engines and visitors understand it.

How long should a title tag be?

Keep it under about 60 characters so it is not cut off in results, and put the main keyword near the start.

What is the difference between AEO and GEO?

AEO makes your content the direct answer in snippets and voice results. GEO makes your brand likely to be cited in answers written by AI tools.

How many keywords should a page target?

One primary keyword plus a small group of closely related secondary keywords that share the same intent.
